# Frequency Bands

Needed to decide what frequency bands to use to create the connectivity graphs. Options included: single band (alpha), full conventional set (delta, theta, alpha, beta, gamma), a reduced subset, or on broadband signal. Also need to decide, if multiple bands are kept, how they enter the graph, ie parallel graphs, stacked edge vectors, fused single graph, etc.

Target is APOEn e4 carrier vs. non-carrier in cognitively healthy middle-aged adults. Not AD vs healthy, which has more literature.

What is directly relevant is Dzaniok's follow up paper. They analyzed the same 79-subject cohort split by APOE/PICALM risk (N, A+P-, A+P+). there were real spectral power effects concentrated in delta (higher in A+P+, widespread) and upper alpha/alpha-2 (lower in A+P+). Theta and lower alpha showed nothing. Beta/gamma weren't tested. Effects mainly driven by the double-risk (A+P+) group, so a pooled carrier vs. non-carrier split will proably show diluted effect sizes. Connectivity (coherence) showed the same directional pattern but didn't survive FDR correction at N-7. Only power measures were robust. So can't expect a single band's wPLI to cleanly separate the groups. Let the GNN pool weak signal across the whole graph, and possibly across bands.

General AD-EEG lit is useful as an upper bound. Shouldn't be taken directly,# since it's built on symptomatic AD comparisons rather than asymptomatic risk. Babiloni et al. (2021) confirms posterior alpha and widespread delta/theta are the most consistent markers, but they also document real beta/gamma involvement. Don't drop gamma. Jelic et al. (1997) found APOE e4 dose effects on EEG in diagnosed AD patients were connectivity-specific rather than power-specific. sugests genotype effects might need a high allele dose to surface and cautions that a heterozygous-inclusive carrier/non-carrier split could show weaker effects.

Two Klepl GNN papers for extra evidence. Their best GNN used the alpha band (baselines preferred theta), no single connectivity measure was consistently best across bands, and a feature-masking ablation found gamma (and theta/low-alpha) masking hurt performance. This is another sign that gamma might have predictive value. Multi-band fusion approaches also outperform single-band graphs on AD-vs-control tasks, supporting multi-band as a reasonable default.

The APOE-specific literature in non-demented people is thinner. Ponomareva et al. (2008) found that plain resting-state EEG showed no genotype differences in non-demented relatives of AD patients; a genotype effect only appeared under a hyperventilation provocation, which PEARL-Neuro's protocol doesn't include. Signal-complexity studies in young healthy APOE e4 carriers do report genotype effects, but they're small and sometimes counter-intuitive in direction. Strongest actual evidence for resting-state APOE effects in a non-demented cohort is your own dataset's companion paper (Dzianok et al., Section 2), which found delta and alpha-2 power differences without any provocation. So, weight the in-cohort result over the general external APOE literature for this decision.

Conclusion: keep all five conventional bands (delta, theta, alpha, beta, gamma), splitting alpha into alpha-1/alpha-2 since own data shows they behave differently. Even bands with no power effect in-cohort (theta, alpha-1) are kept because power and connectivity don't necessarily carry signal in the same bands, and the underpowered in-cohort connectivity analysis may be masking effects a GNN could still pool across the graph. Gamma stays in too. Treat as an something to settle by ablation (single-band vs. full 5-band stack vs. continuous full-spectrum features) not something that can be fully decided by literature, since most of that literature is built on a different, higher-effect-size population. Practically, multiple bands should enter the graph as a per-band wPLI adjacency matrix folded into a single edge feature vector per subject, rather than as separate graphs — one graph per subject, band-resolved at the edge level.

GIVEN TIME CONSTRAINTS, CHOOSE: full 5-band stack

# UPDATE

The original version of this section recommended combining bands by giving each edge a length-5 vector of per-band wPLI values on a single shared graph. Overriding this. Researching how GNN architectures actually consume edge information (see edge-features.md) showed that standard GCN (default architecture showing Klepl et al. (2022)'s precedent) only accepts a single scalar weight per edge. Kipf & Welling's own paper says this directly as a limitation, this is backed by Gong & Cheng (2019). A vector-valued edge would have require switching to a fundamentally different, less precedented layer type like GINEConv, NNConv or EGNN, which is an architecture decision, and not flagged at the time of originally reseraching this. The new decision, decided in edge-features.md, keeps the same underlying goal. This is that the GNN sees all bands, band-resolved, but routes it differently. There is one graph per band, each processed through its own GCN branch with ordinary scalar wPLI edges, with branch outputs concatenated after graph convolution rather than fused at the edge level. This lines up with Xu et al. (2025)'s architecture directly (closest task-matched precedent available) and preserves the exact same band-resolved information the original proposal wanted, just carried through parallel convolution passes instead of a single richer edge.
