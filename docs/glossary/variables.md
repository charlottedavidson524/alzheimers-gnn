Red blood cell measurements:

- 'erythrocytes': Red blood cell counts (the cells that carry oxygen). Typical units: ×10^12/L.

- 'hemoglobin': Concentration of oxygen-carrying protein inside red-blood cells. This is the main quantity referred to when speaking about iron levels. Typical units: g/dL or g/L.

- 'hematocrit': The percentage of blood volume that is red cells. This is closely related to hemoglobin and erythrocytes. Typical units: % or fraction.

- 'MCV': Mean Corpuscular Volume. This is the average size of a single red blood cell. It's useful for diagnosing types of aneamia. (small cells vs large cells). Typical units: femtolitres (fL).

- 'MCH': Mean Corpuscular Haemoglobin. Average amount of haemoglobin in a single red cell. Typical units: picograms (pg)

- 'MCHC': Mean Corpuscular Haemoglobin Concentration. Hemoglobin per unit cell volume (MCH/MCV, roughly speaking). Typical units: g/dL.

- 'RDW-CV': Red cell Distribution Width, Coefficient of Variation. Measures how variable red blood sizes are within the sample. Elevated RDW is a non-specific marker linked with many conditions including cardiovascular risk and cognitive decline. Typical units are percentage.

Platelet measurements:

- 'platelets': Platelet counts. These are the cell fragments involved in clotting. Typical units: ×10^9/L.

- 'PDW': Platelet Distribution Width. Variability in platelet size. Typical unitsare percentage.

- 'MPV': Mean Platelet Volume. Average platelet size. Larger platelets are usually more reactive. Typical units: fL.

- 'P-LCR': Platelt Large Cell Ratio. Proportion of platelets larger than 12 fL. Related to MPV. Typical units: percentage.

White blood cell measurements:

White blood cells split into five sub-types. Each are reported as an absolute counts (×10^9/L) and a percentage of total leukocytes. The two encodings carry the same info and including both in a mdoel is redundant.

- 'leukocytes': Total white blood cell counts. These are the cells of the immune system. Typical units: ×10^9/L.

- 'neutrophils': Most abundant white cell. First line response to bacterial infection.

- 'lymphocytes': Adaptive immunity. T cells, B ceells, NK cells. Core to viral defence and long-term immune memory.

- 'monocytes': Tissue macrophage precursors. Involved in inflammation and clearing debris.

- 'eosinophils': Parastitic infection response and allergic reactions.

- 'basophils': Histomine release in allergic/inflammatory responses. These are the rarest white blood cell.

Won't list neutrophils*%, lymphocytes*%, etc due to redundancy.

Lipid panel:

This is a standard cardiovascular risk assessment.

- 'total_cholesterol': Sum of all cholestrol carrying particles in blood.

- 'cholesterol_HDL': High-Density Lipoprotein cholesterol. What is known as "good cholesterol" because HDL particles transport cholesterol away from arterial walls back to the liver. Higher is better.

- 'non-HDL_cholesterol': total cholesterol minus HDL. Captures all artery-damaging cholesterol-carrying
  particles in one number. Linked to other columns so could be redundant.

- 'LDL_cholesterol': Low-Density Lipoprotein cholesterol. Known as "bad cholesterol" because LDL particles deposit
  cholesterol into arterial walls. Higher count means higher cardiovascular risk. APOE e4 carriers tend to have higher LDL.

- 'triglycerides': circulating fats stored for energy. Higher triglycerides are associated with metabolic syndrome and cardiovascular risk.

These variables are all likely intrerelated. Not good for linear models but fine for tree-based models or GNNs. There could be signal here due to APOE e4 being associated with higher LDL and total cholestrol (Xu et al., 2023)

Infection marker:

- 'HSV_r': Binary indicator (0 = no, 1 = yes) of a positive IgG antibody result for herpes simplex virus (HSV). IgG indicates lifetime exposure (past or chronic infection) rather than active infection. HSV-1 and HSV-2 are not distinguished.
